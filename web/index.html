How far has housing in Portugal outrun incomes?

Official house price, rent, consumer price and income statistics for Portugal, six peer countries and the EU average, modelled in SQL with dbt and checked by automated tests.

Finding 1House prices in Portugal have tripled since 2015.

The index reached 301 in the second quarter of 2026 (2015 = 100), the steepest rise of the eight economies here, and was still up 16.5% on a year earlier. After consumer inflation, prices are 2.3 times their 2015 level.

Finding 2Prices have outrun incomes faster than in any peer.

From 2015 to 2024 house prices rose 124% while median household income rose 65%. The price-to-income index is 136, against 131 in the Netherlands, 118 in Ireland and 106 for the EU.

Finding 3Building has not caught up.

Permits for 40,900 homes were issued in 2025, almost six times the 2014 low, but still 45% below the 73,900 of 2005. Rents, at +43% since 2015, have risen less than house prices.

Finding 4The average household is not where the pressure shows.

Only 6.3% of people in Portugal spend over 40% of income on housing, below the EU's 7.7%, because most households own their home outright. The squeeze falls on buyers and new tenants, which national averages hide.

How the numbers are built

  • Data: Eurostat, downloaded through its statistics API: house price index (prc_hpi_q), harmonised consumer prices including actual rentals (prc_hicp_minr), housing cost overburden rate (ilc_lvho07a), median equivalised net income (ilc_di03) and building permits (sts_cobp_a). The HICP series is the ECOICOP ver. 2 table that replaced prc_hicp_midx in 2026.
  • Model: dbt on DuckDB. Staging views type and filter the raw tables; marts form a star schema (dim_country, dim_quarter, fct_house_prices, fct_consumer_prices, fct_living_conditions, fct_building_permits) with tests for keys, relationships, accepted values and a check that every index averages 100 in its base year.
  • Real prices = house price index ÷ all-items HICP × 100. Price-to-income = annual average house price index ÷ median income indexed to 2015 × 100.
  • Caveats: the rent index covers all tenancies, including old contracts, so it rises more slowly than asking rents for new leases. EU-SILC asks about the previous year's income, so incomes are aligned to their income year before they are compared with prices; the latest price-to-income year is therefore 2024. These are national figures; Lisbon and the Algarve sit well above them.
